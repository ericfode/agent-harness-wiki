## Editorial synthesis

The publishable movement clusters around six themes:

1. `testing-rl` remains the clearest live verifier/test-generation signal, with `testing-rl-hermes` and `is-it-formal` as smaller companion prototypes;
2. Basis-style specification work is broadening toward durable provenance services through `basis`, `basis-hermes`, and `steward`, while `basis-jcode` remains too artifact-heavy tonight;
3. Gemma/tinygrad work has both a substantial public-facing package (`tinygrad-gemma`) and several optimization/replay sandboxes that should not be over-publicized;
4. NNPL remains most credible where it preserves baselines, typed boundaries, and negative results;
5. orchestration repos are rich but often dirty, internal, or artifact-heavy, so public copy should emphasize architecture and withhold run state;
6. craft projects earn front-page treatment the old way: a clean worktree, license, manifest, docs, and tests. `handterm` is the tidy little theorem here.

A public note can say that much. It should not say more merely because the filesystem was candid.
